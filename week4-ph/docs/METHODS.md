## 2.x  A curated pH benchmark for plastic-degrading enzymes

### 2.x.1  Sources

The pH benchmark is a subset of an independent, test-only benchmark of plastic-degrading
enzyme performance assembled from open-access primary literature in Europe PMC. That
parent collection mined 558 open-access articles and shipped 606 verified measurements
across temperature, pH and electrolyte axes; 105 of those rows recorded a pH, and those
105 form the candidate pool for the work described here.

Every candidate value was measured in a laboratory and reported in a peer-reviewed
open-access article. No value is synthetic, predicted, interpolated or model-generated.
Each row travels with the verbatim sentence it was read from, plus the article's PMCID,
PubMed ID and DOI, so any reader can return to the source.

Articles already mined by earlier phases of the project, and every article cited by any
of the seven shared training datasets, were excluded **before** full text was retrieved,
so the same measurement cannot re-enter the benchmark under a different enzyme name.

After curation the benchmark comprises **67 measurements from 30 open-access articles**
published between 2015 and 2026.

### 2.x.2  Inclusion criteria

A candidate row was retained only if all of the following held.

1. **The value is a measurement, not a protocol detail.** Assay buffers, incubation
   temperatures, the range of conditions screened, and the baselines used to normalise
   relative activity were all excluded, even when they are numerically correct
   statements about the experiment.
2. **The value is first-hand.** Sentences restating another study's result, and articles
   that are reviews or editorials, were excluded.
3. **The value is attributable to one enzyme.** Where a sentence reports different values
   for two or more enzymes, the row was excluded unless the evidence assigns the recorded
   value unambiguously to a named enzyme. Where several enzymes are reported to *share* a
   single value, the row was retained and flagged
   (`attribution_certainty = multiple_enzymes_same_value`).
4. **The enzyme is in scope.** The benchmark covers carboxylester hydrolases
   (EC 3.1.1.–: esterases, lipases, cutinases, feruloyl and acetyl xylan esterases,
   PETases, carboxylesterases) and polymer depolymerases (PHA, PHB, PCL, PVC), together
   with the nylon and phthalate hydrolases that act on non-ester synthetic polymers and
   plasticisers. Glycoside hydrolases, polysaccharide lyases, monooxygenases and
   non-enzymatic chemistry were excluded even where a polymer appears elsewhere in the
   article — for instance an invertase immobilised *on* a poly(hydroxybutyrate) support,
   where the polymer is the carrier and not the substrate.
   Feruloyl and acetyl xylan esterases are retained deliberately: they are carboxylester
   hydrolases, and PET46 — an archaeal feruloyl esterase that hydrolyses PET — is in the
   benchmark itself.
5. **The value is not a prediction.** One candidate reported a response-surface optimum
   (*"The model predicted maximum biodegradation at pH 7.5"*) and was removed.
6. **The evidence is machine-readable as evidence.** Concatenated table cells with no
   recoverable field boundaries were excluded.

Of 105 candidates, **38 (36.2%) failed one or more criteria** and were removed;
14 of the 44 contributing articles lost every row they had supplied.

### 2.x.3  Cleaning and correction

Rows that passed the inclusion criteria were corrected where the source sentence
supported a better reading. Corrections only ever move a field to something the article
itself states; the sentence travels with the row so each call is checkable.

| Correction | Rows | What it does |
|---|---:|---|
| Direction recorded | 54 | whether activity was *held* or *lost* at that pH |
| Enzyme named or corrected | 52 | the enzyme the evidence names is written into the row |
| Outcome recovered | 29 | the residual or relative activity **at** that pH lifted into its own field |
| pH interval bounds set | 18 | `pH_low` / `pH_high` set to the stated range |
| Measurement type reclassified | 16 | e.g. a maximal-activity statement typed as a pH optimum rather than pH stability |
| pH optimum corrected | 5 | optimum reset from a range endpoint to the stated optimum |
| Incompatible buffer cleared | 4 | a buffer the evidence does not name and that cannot hold the recorded pH |

Two correction classes are worth stating explicitly in the manuscript.

**Range endpoints were being reported as optima.** Five rows named a pH optimum that was
in fact the low end of an activity interval, while the same sentence gave the true
optimum — for example *"activity across a wide pH spectrum, ranging from pH 5 to pH 9,
with optimal activity observed at pH 7"*, recorded as an optimum of pH 5. The corrections
were 5→7, 4.0→5.0, 7.5→8.0, 7.0→8.0 and 7.0→7.5: errors of 0.5–2.0 pH units in precisely
the quantity a screener is asked to predict.

**Direction was missing from stability rows.** A row reading `pH stability = 10.0` is
ambiguous between "stable at pH 10" and "loses activity at pH 10", and both occur in the
corpus. Direction is now recorded on 54 of 67 rows and the residual activity itself on 29.

### 2.x.4  Duplicate removal

Duplicates were removed at two levels.

*Upstream*, the parent benchmark screened every candidate against an index built from all
seven shared datasets, the project's Week-2 training dataset, the Week-1 pH database and
the Week-1 conditions database — 970,289 protein sequences, 16,297 accessions, 6,941
paper identifiers and 1,579,354 measurement fingerprints — and removed 77 internal
duplicates before the pH subset was drawn.

*Within the pH subset*, a duplicate is a row reporting the same enzyme, article,
measurement type and pH as a row already retained. Three such rows were removed; in each
case the surviving row came from the article's direct statement of the result and the
removed row from a later restatement in a methods or kinetics sentence.

Rows that state the two ends of a single pH interval are **not** treated as duplicates.
They are kept as separate endpoint rows — matching the one-row-per-endpoint convention of
the project's existing pH database — and linked by a shared `ph_range_group`, so the
interval can be reassembled without losing the ability to join row-for-row.

A final automated check confirms that no two scored rows share
`(article, enzyme, measurement type, pH)`.

### 2.x.5  Units and standardisation

pH is dimensionless and needs no conversion, so unit handling here is about
*representation* rather than arithmetic.

| Quantity | Canonical form | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| pH | dimensionless, one decimal | point values in `pH`; intervals additionally in `pH_low`/`pH_high` with `pH_is_range = yes` |
| pH interval | two endpoint rows | linked by `ph_range_group`; no midpoint is ever synthesised |
| Outcome at pH | % of maximal or initial activity | `relative_activity_pct`, with `relative_activity_qualifier` ∈ {exact, approx, gte, lte, range} preserving *"about 80%"*, *"over 90%"*, *"less than 50%"* |
| Direction | controlled vocabulary | optimum / stabilising / destabilising |
| Temperature | °C | inherited from the parent pipeline (K and °F converted there) |
| Exposure time | minutes | converted from s, h, d, weeks |
| Buffer concentration | mM | converted from M, %(w/v), mg/mL, g/L |

Two rules follow from this and are enforced by the validation suite. First, **no midpoint
is ever stored**: an interval reported as pH 4.0–6.0 is never collapsed to 5.0, because
5.0 is a number the article did not report. Second, **a qualifier is never silently
dropped**: *"less than 50%"* is stored as `50` with qualifier `lte`, never as a bare `50`.

### 2.x.6  Validation

The dataset is rebuilt and re-validated by a single command
(`python3 scripts/validate_ph.py`), which runs 22 checks — 19 hard, 3 advisory — and
exits non-zero on any hard failure. All 19 hard checks pass on the shipped data. One
advisory check reports a known and accepted condition: 9 scored rows carry neither an
enzyme name nor an accession, because the article's own sentence names no enzyme. Those
rows remain usable for condition-axis analysis and are excluded from sequence-model
scoring by construction.

| Check | What it guarantees |
|---|---|
| pH within 0–14 | no parsing artefacts |
| bounds ordered; `pH_low ≤ pH ≤ pH_high` | intervals are coherent |
| `value_std` equals `pH` for pH-typed rows | the canonical convention holds |
| **the recorded pH appears in the evidence sentence** | the value was not introduced downstream |
| relative activity in 0–200%, low ≤ high | outcomes are physically possible |
| controlled vocabularies for type, direction, qualifier, tier | no free text in categorical fields |
| PMCID, DOI and evidence present on every row | provenance is complete |
| `protein_id` recomputes from the sequence with Luke's hash | the join key is not merely copied |
| sequences contain only amino-acid letters | no truncated or corrupt records |
| no prediction language in any shipped evidence sentence | the integrity statement holds |
| no duplicate scored `(article, enzyme, type, pH)` | deduplication held |
| range groups internally consistent; both bounds present | intervals reassemble |
| every row source-verified upstream | the parent pipeline's check still applies |
| *(advisory)* buffer can hold the recorded pH | catches buffers attached from elsewhere |
| *(advisory)* stability rows carry a direction | catches silent ambiguity |

The buffer-compatibility check earns its place: it is what surfaced MES — which buffers
pH 5.5–6.7 — attached to rows recorded at pH 7 and pH 8. Those buffers were not named in
the evidence and have been cleared.

### 2.x.7  Independence from training data

Luke's handoff specifies the check: recompute each benchmark sequence's `protein_id` as
`"P" + sha1(sequence.upper().replace("*",""))[:12]` and confirm that none appears with
`split_homology == "train"` in `all_unique_proteins_homology.csv`. This was re-run over
the pH subset alone (`scripts/check_overlap_luke.py`) rather than inherited from the
parent build.

Of 6 distinct proteins: **0 fall in the training split**, 1 (SbPETase, `P58458fbad1df`)
sits in the held-out test split and is tiered `B` accordingly, and 5 appear nowhere in
the project's existing data. The benchmark is released for final external testing only —
not for training, tuning, feature selection or threshold selection.

### 2.x.8  Availability

Data, code and the full audit trail are in `week4-ph/`. The benchmark is provided in its
native schema (`data/ph_benchmark_v1.csv`), in the project's standardized 24-column
spreadsheet format (`data/ph_benchmark_luke_format.csv`) and as an indexed SQLite
database with pre-built scoring views. `data/ph_audit_log.csv` records a verdict, a rule
and a reason for all 105 candidates, including the 38 that were removed.

## Suggested manuscript sentences

For the Results, where the benchmark is first described:

> We assembled an independent pH benchmark of 67 experimentally measured values from 30
> open-access articles, spanning pH 3.0–12.0 and 19 enzyme classes. Every value was read
> against the source sentence it was extracted from, and 38 of 105 candidate rows (36%)
> were removed for reasons recorded per row — most commonly because the value came from a
> protocol sentence rather than a result (13 rows), the enzyme was out of scope (8), or
> the sentence restated another study (7). No benchmark protein appears in the training
> split.

For the Discussion, on what the audit implies:

> The defects we removed were not random noise. They were systematic and directional:
> range endpoints reported as optima, protocol conditions reported as results, and
> stability values shipped without the sign of the effect. A pattern-extracted dataset
> that has been verified only for *whether a number appears in the article* will contain
> all three, because each defect is a correct quotation placed in the wrong field. This
> argues for reporting extraction benchmarks with the evidence sentence attached, and for
> treating "value located in source" and "value correctly attributed" as two separate
> quality metrics.
