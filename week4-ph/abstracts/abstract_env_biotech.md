**Reaction pH in enzymatic plastic degradation: a curated benchmark across seven polymer
classes, and the gap between published conditions and process conditions**

A. Goel, L. ——, A. ——, S. —— · Predictive Enzyme Technology Laboratory

---

Enzymatic depolymerisation is moving from demonstration to process, and pH is one of the
few operating variables that is cheap to control and strongly determines both enzyme
performance and the rate of competing chemical hydrolysis. Predictive screeners that rank
candidate enzymes for a given process therefore need condition-resolved performance data
— and need to be tested on data they have never seen.

We assembled an independent, test-only pH benchmark for plastic-active hydrolases: 67
experimentally measured values from 31 open-access articles, spanning pH 3.0–12.0 and 19
enzyme classes acting on PET and its monomers, polyhydroxyalkanoates,
poly(hydroxybutyrate), poly(ε-caprolactone), poly(vinyl chloride), poly(lactic acid),
nylon and the plasticiser DEHP. No protein in the benchmark appears in the screener's
training data, verified against the training split protein by protein.

Two results are relevant beyond our own modelling.

First, **coverage does not match process conditions.** Reported optima skew alkaline
(median pH 8.0), which is consistent with base-favoured polyester hydrolysis, but only 22
records report pH and temperature together, and none sits in the alkaline, high-
temperature regime where industrial PET depolymerisation operates. The condition space
that matters most industrially is the least characterised in the open literature.

Second, **published condition data is less reliable than its verification implies.** All
105 candidate records had been automatically confirmed to appear verbatim in their source
articles. Reading each against that sentence still removed 37 (35%): values taken from
assay protocols rather than results, activity-range endpoints reported as optima, enzymes
outside the relevant catalytic classes, review articles restating other groups' results,
and one response-surface prediction. Curation also recovered the activity actually
retained at each pH for 29 records and the direction of the effect for 53 — information
routinely lost when a pH value is stored without its outcome, leaving a stability record
that cannot distinguish alkali tolerance from alkali destruction.

Data, per-record evidence and the complete audit trail are released.
